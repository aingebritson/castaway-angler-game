# The Cayes research dossier

Species pass: 17 November 2026 (spec section 12). The 14 species first added for this region were checked against live sources, and all 52 of their source references are now read and dated. The 50 occurrence records at the four Cayes spots were then checked too. Every source in the game's content is now read and dated; none is `pending-review`. Island species that reappear here were checked in `research/castaway-island/`.

## Overview

Port Esperanza is a lightly fictional fishing town on the Belize coast (17.25°N 88.0°W, `America/Belize`), reached by raft from the island (DECISIONS 17). It has four fishing spots:

| Spot | Zones | Real habitat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Town Pier | pilings, harbour, bait school | Harbour pier and its shade; sardine schools for the cast net |
| Mangrove Creek | roots, creek | Red mangrove prop roots and a deep creek bend |
| Patch Reef | reef top, reef edge, deep | Inner-lagoon patch reef with a ledge into deeper water |
| The Cut | channel, blue edge, blue water | A tidal channel through the barrier reef and the drop beyond it |

## What changed

### Sizes and length-weight parameters

The rule is the same as for the island: weight at maximum length must be within 30% of the record. The record is the IGFA all-tackle record where one could be found, otherwise FishBase's largest published weight. IGFA's own database is for members only, so the IGFA figures come from published reports (medium confidence).

### Conservation and law

All IUCN statuses match FishBase (Red List version 2025-2): Nassau grouper CR, black grouper NT, hogfish VU, queen triggerfish NT, nurse shark VU, West Indian manatee VU, and the rest LC or not evaluated.

- **Nassau grouper:** release-only in the game. Belize actually allows it from April to November within a 51 to 76 cm slot, and closes the fishery from December to March. The note now says so. Making it release-only goes further than Belize law, which is defensible for a Critically Endangered fish.
- **Manatee:** Belize holds the largest share of the Endangered Antillean subspecies (EDGE of Existence). This confirms the note.

**Checked and kept as written:** white grunt "kissing" and throat-teeth grunting; snook's black lateral line; crevalle croaking when caught; black grouper starting life female; hogfish's pig-like snout, harem and trailing dorsal spines; king mackerel's sharply dipping lateral line and seasonal migrations; blue crab's paddle legs and red-tipped female claws; manatee's daily intake and replaced molars; nurse shark's nocturnal feeding.

### Sources fixed

- **Florida Museum links:** they used scientific-name slugs that do not exist. All eight now point to the real common-name pages, and all eight were read.
- **IGFA entries:** unsourced "IGFA world records" entries were replaced by the reports the records came from.
- **Manatee status:** read from Wikipedia, because the Red List page refuses automated reads.

### Simulator effect

I ran the simulator at the four spots with the deckhand, skipper and master rigs, in January and June. Catch shares moved by under 2 points, and coins per hour by under 4%. The Town Pier, where the first shop rod's price was tuned (DECISIONS 18), changed by under 1%. Gray snapper, Nassau grouper and crevalle jack now reach their larger maximums.

## Occurrence pass

The 50 occurrences at the four Cayes spots were checked. Island species (tarpon, permit, bonefish, barracuda, mutton and yellowtail snapper, spotted moray, houndfish and the rest) now carry the values the island research settled on, except where the Cayes spot itself justifies a difference. The new species were checked against the Florida Museum, FishBase, NOAA, guide pages and study excerpts.

### Months

| Species | Before | After | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| King mackerel | Winter peak (1.4–1.5 from December to March), summer low (0.6) | **Spring peak**: 1.4 to 1.5 from April to June, lowest (0.8) in September; confidence low | Belize charter pages and an Ambergris Caye species list put kingfish at their best from April to June. The winter peak is the Florida Keys mixing zone (NOAA). |
| Nassau grouper | Winter peak at Patch Reef (1.6–1.8) | **Flat 1.0** | Nassau grouper spawning aggregations form from November to February at a few reef promontories (NOAA). A patch reef in the lagoon is not one of them, and the fish leave home reefs to spawn. |
| Black grouper | Winter peak (1.3) | **Flat 1.0**, confidence low | No Belize source for a season. The pattern matched the Florida barracuda curve. |
| Tarpon, permit, bonefish, barracuda, houndfish, mutton snapper, yellowtail snapper | Florida-style curves | Same as the island | See the island dossier |
| Common snook | Peak June to August | Unchanged | Belize guides: resident all year, best in the warm months when they spawn (FishBase: at passes, May to September) |

**Confirmed as written:**
- **Night feeders:** gray snapper adults, white grunt (off the reef at night), schoolmaster, squirrelfish, nurse shark, octopus, lobster. Blue crabs are also denser in the shallows at night.
- **Day feeders:** crevalle jack, hogfish and queen triggerfish (which wedges into crevices at night).
- **Snook** in the shadow line of lights after dark.
- **Manatee** grazing up to eight hours a day.

### Rumours affected

- *king-winter* said the big kings "come down the coast in the cool months". That is the Florida migration. It now says "The big kings show off the deep ledge and the channel mouth in spring, April into June", and marks April to June.
- *nassau-winter* is still true: Nassau grouper do gather on the winter moons, at a few reef points. But it marked December to February as the months to look, and the patch reef is now flat. It keeps its story and no longer marks any month.

### Sources fixed

- The Florida Museum links in the Cayes occurrences also used scientific-name slugs; all now point to the real pages.
- Five "guide fishing reports (general knowledge)" entries were replaced by named pages. Snook and tarpon at dock lights are from *Salt Water Sportsman* and *Sport Fishing*; the months are from Aardvark McLeod and FishingBooker.

### Simulator effect

The same runs as the species pass:
- **Town Pier:** unchanged, so the first-rod pricing (DECISIONS 18) still holds.
- **Patch Reef and the Cut:** coins per hour fall by up to 13% in January and rise by up to 9% in June. Winter loses the Nassau and king mackerel peaks, and spring gains the king mackerel peak.
- **Rare-fish share** at Patch Reef shifts the same way, a few points down in winter and up in early summer.

## Open questions

1. **Nassau grouper release-only vs Belize's slot.** The game is stricter than the law. If the Cayes market should follow Belize exactly, Nassau grouper would be sellable from April to November between 51 and 76 cm.
2. **White grunt misses the 30% check** against FishBase's 4.4 kg maximum weight. The Florida Museum repeats that figure, but every published length-weight set gives 1.9–2.5 kg at 53 cm, and state rod records are about 2.4 kg. This is the same situation as the island's yellowfin mojarra. I kept the Carolinas set.
3. **Cero maximum length.** FishBase and the Florida Museum both give 183 cm TL, which is implausible for a 7.8 kg fish; it looks like a king mackerel figure. The game uses 90 cm FL, chosen so the only fork-length set passes the weight check. This needs a better source.
4. **Nurse shark maximum.** FishBase gives 430 cm TL. The game keeps 308 cm, the largest size widely reported. This is a sighting, so it only affects flavour.
5. **King mackerel maximum (173 cm FL)** has no direct source. NOAA gives "up to 5.5 feet" (168 cm), and FishBase gives 184 cm TL.
